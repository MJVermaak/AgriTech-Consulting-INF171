Data ethics

Four principles for agricultural data

Collecting data does not give an organisation unrestricted rights to use or share it. Smallholders often depend on larger partners, so fairness has to be designed in.

Ownership & control

A data governance agreement should state who may access each data type, for what purpose, for how long, and when it can be shared.

  • Farmers can view records about their operations
  • Clear rights over non-personal data such as aggregated yields
  • Route to raise concerns about misuse

Transparency & consent

Privacy notices in plain language, translated for members. Consent must be informed, specific and freely given, but it is not the only lawful basis under POPIA.

  • Explain purposes before collecting
  • No hidden terms or misleading explanations
  • Space for questions

Fairness

Production costs and alternative buyers can shift negotiating power. Data must not be turned against the people who supplied it.

  • Transparent, consistent purchasing criteria
  • Audit automated recommendations for bias
  • No sharing of individual commercial data with competitors

Accuracy & accountability

Recording 500 crates instead of 50 can trigger wasted capacity, false commitments to retailers and payment disputes.

  • Validate at the point of entry
  • Log corrections and name an owner for data quality
  • A designated contact for complaints
South African law

POPIA: the eight conditions

The Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 applies whenever identifiable people, such as farmers, drivers, employees or individual retailers, are involved. Anonymous aggregated yields may fall outside it. A farm location that identifies a person may not.

Accountability

Assign responsibility for compliance.

Processing limitation

Lawful, fair, minimal collection.

Purpose specification

State why data is collected: harvest planning, delivery, payment.

Further processing

Delivery data should not quietly become marketing data.

Information quality

Keep records complete and current.

Openness

Tell farmers what is collected and who receives it.

Security safeguards

Technical and organisational protection (s19).

Data subject participation

Access, correction and deletion where applicable.

Who is responsible for what?

Responsible party

Decides the purpose and means of processing. Typically the cooperative and/or processor, depending on the arrangement.

Operator

Processes on someone's behalf, such as a platform or cloud provider. POPIA requires a written contract imposing security measures.

Cooperative

Keeps member records, explains data practices.

Processor

Uses production and delivery data for planning and fulfilment.

Retailers

See availability, quantities, delivery dates and traceability only.

Farmers' rights: request access, correct errors, object to certain processing, withdraw consent where it is the basis, request deletion where legally applicable, and complain. Deletion is not unconditional, since some records must be kept for legal, accounting or food-safety reasons. Cross-border transfers (e.g. foreign cloud hosting) need their own POPIA check.
